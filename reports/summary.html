How predictable is an NFL offense before the snap?

Calling run vs pass on unseen seasons from pre-snap information, and measuring how much each offense gives away beyond league norms, with honest sample sizes.

Andres Perez, M.S. Kinesiology (Biomechanics)R · xgboost · lme4 · ShinyCode: github.com/Andresperez397/nfl-presnap-predictability

Data

342,254 offensive plays from 2016–2025 (nflverse play-by-play, participation and FTN charting). Inputs are things a defense sees before the snap: down, distance, field position, clock, score, timeouts, shotgun, no-huddle and personnel. Test seasons are 2019–2025.

Approach

  • Audit, then a frozen plan: every field was classed as pre- or post-snap; a 2023 data-source change was handled; the plan was committed before modeling.
  • Temporal validation: train on earlier seasons, test on the next; team models predict each week from earlier weeks only.
  • Shrinkage: team tendencies are random effects (lme4) on top of the league model.
  • Uncertainty: game-cluster bootstrap, 1,000 resamples.

Findings

  • Three plays in four: 74.5% accuracy (AUC 0.82) on unseen seasons, against 61.1% for always guessing pass and 62.5% for down and distance alone. The model is well calibrated.
  • Shrink or lose: a team's own tendencies, shrunk, improve week-ahead prediction by 8.2 millinats per play. Raw team rates are 109 worse than ignoring teams.
  • A stable trait: the tendency index has split-half r = 0.74 and year-to-year r = 0.43. The most scoutable offenses are run-heavy teams with mobile quarterbacks.
  • Not a cost: more scoutable seasons had higher EPA per play within franchise (an association).
  • Formation matters: charted pistol and backfield count add 33 millinats per play (2024–25).

Limitations

Consistent formation detail exists only from 2022, so earlier seasons rely on the shotgun flag and personnel, and the main model understates predictability. Personnel uses roster positions. The efficiency link is confounded by quarterback and roster quality, game script and coaching. App intervals treat the league model as known. Scouting tables are descriptive; every predictive claim comes from held-out tests.
